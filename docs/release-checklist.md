## Which identities and devices are needed?

**Apple is needed for the configured end-to-end native sign-in test, not for local database tests.** `eas.json` enables Apple; other provider flags are absent. `onboardingAuth.ts` obtains an Apple identity token on iOS and exchanges it with Supabase. Real acceptance must exercise this route, including nonce and hosted session handling. Mock tokens/local Auth rows cannot demonstrate provider configuration. The [Expo Apple authentication reference](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/apple-authentication/) also calls for device testing.

**Existing accounts can reduce setup.** The owner may designate two existing, controlled Apple identities and already-provisioned devices; no new Apple ID, developer credential, device registration or provider setting is inherently required. Existing Kavanah accounts qualify only if designated test accounts contain synthetic data and exact permitted changes are agreed. Signing in with an Apple identity not yet linked to Kavanah can create a hosted Auth user; reusing an identity does not make the operation read-only. Do not use customer records or ordinary personal history as fixtures, collect passwords/tokens in chat, or delete a pre-existing account under the proposed cleanup scope. Destructive account deletion remains limited to newly created disposable Kavanah fixtures explicitly approved for deletion.

**Two devices do not prove same-device switching.** They can cover Circle interactions and each identity's sign-in/restart. They cannot establish A→B→A isolation on one installation. The inspected native Apple path has no in-app alternate-Apple-account selector, and an approved, already-supported switch route has not been established. Do not change the device's system Apple account, enable another provider or inject/export hosted tokens to claim this gate passed. Keep it open until the test route is agreed and verified.

**Synthetic local identities already cover substantial logic.** Existing fixtures test RLS/private/anonymous denial, account-required guards, sharing/block/deletion invariants, duplicate/retry handling, quota concurrency, local account storage/delayed callbacks and isolated dump/restore with deletion only in the restored copy. These tests need no Apple sign-in, hosted Auth user, real data, provider credentials or expanded access. They cannot establish real Auth/PostgREST delivery, Apple entitlements, installed SecureStore/MMKV/SQLite behavior, OS permissions/accessibility or recovery of the actual provider backup.
