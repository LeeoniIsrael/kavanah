# Siddur edition decision

The implemented library navigation is independent of the eventual approved edition. It reuses the existing Sefaria-backed reader and license gates; it does not certify the imported texts.

## Source research

- Sefaria lists Ashkenaz, Sefard and Edot HaMizrach as separate works: https://www.sefaria.org/texts/Liturgy/Siddur . Existing app metadata identifies reusable editions, but reusable licensing alone establishes no rabbinic approval of Kavanah's rendering or completeness.
- Rabbi Rallis Wiesenthal's Tefiloh Sefas Yisroel is published by Open Siddur under CC BY-SA 4.0 and follows the specific Bad Homburg custom: https://opensiddur.org/compilations/liturgical/siddurim/kol-bo/siddur-bnei-ashkenaz-a-german-rite-siddur-prepared-by-r-rallis-wiesenthal/ . It should not silently be substituted for a general Ashkenaz siddur; rabbinic authorship alone is not a documented endorsement of this app.
- Koren offers established Orthodox editions, including the Koren Sacks/Shalem family: https://korenpub.com/collections/siddurim/ashkenaz . No app redistribution permission was established during this task. Do not import a retail edition merely because a PDF or website is readable.

## Required before claiming full rabbinic approval

Select the exact edition and tradition with the user. Obtain applicable digital rights and its documented rabbinic approval. Import exact source text, instructions and attribution without AI-generated liturgical additions. Have the app rendering, seasonal inserts, Israel/diaspora differences, individual/minyan passages, and men's/women's variants reviewed against that edition. Verify completeness, offline availability and all source-to-screen navigation mappings. Keep approval scoped to the reviewer, edition and version actually examined.

For product scope, retain Siddur and Find a prayer in the existing Prayer tab. Machzor, Haggadah, Tehillim, and Megillot can later live inside the same library when a user needs them; they do not need new bottom tabs.
